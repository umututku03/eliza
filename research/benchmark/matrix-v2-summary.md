**The run is deterministic.** The final reruns of D1, H2 and H3 are byte-identical to the earlier runs (same SHA-256).

**Threat model.** The agent's key is stolen or prompt-injected. It holds every delegation the user signed and sends any execution straight to the chain, bypassing the framework and its confirmation gate. This is the worst case for an agent, so it bounds what any agent bug can do.

**Cases.** 18 per cell, each from an isolated snapshot: fresh (S0), after step 1 (S1), or after both steps (S2).
- **Off-plan (15 or 16):**
  - repeats of step 1 and step 2;
  - step 2 before step 1;
  - exfiltration: sale output to the attacker, ETH to the attacker, all X to the attacker, an attacker approval, step-2 output to the attacker;
  - a swap into an asset the plan never names;
  - reserve breaches (overspend, spend all X);
  - goal misses;
  - B2 (the goal's number as the input amount), when it violates the constraints;
  - spending the budget fixed at signing, when it violates the constraints.
- **Legitimate (2 or 3):** step 1, and the feasible step 2 (reach the goal while keeping the reserve).

Labels for step-2 amounts follow from the constraints at S1, not from the amount's name.

**Layers:**
- `none`: the agent's key holds the funds.
- `per_tx_policy`: stateless target, method, recipient and value caps, plus relative balance-change caps on step 2.
- `existing_controls`: per_tx_policy plus LimitedCalls = 1 on every step.
- `pg_floor_only`: the BalanceFloorEnforcer floors only (ablation).
- `pg_no_floor`: PlanStepEnforcer only (ablation).
- `plan_guard`: both enforcers.

All guarded layers keep the target, method and recipient caveats.

**States:**
- `calibrated`.
- `shortfall`: step 1 delivers 3% less.
- `prefunded`: the account holds the step-2 budget before step 1.
- `price_worse`: prefunded, and Y is 12% dearer before step 2, so the signing-time budget misses the goal while the actual surplus reaches it.

## Reading

- **plan_guard** admitted 0 of 244 off-plan actions and refused 0 of 44 legitimate ones, on all four tasks and in all four states.
- **existing_controls** (our plan built from MetaMask's built-in caveats: call limits plus relative balance caps) admitted 16 and refused 4 legitimate ones:
  - under shortfall, the reserve-breaking overspend and the signing-time budget (8);
  - under prefunding, step 2 before step 1 (4 + 4);
  - under price_worse, it also refused the feasible step 2 (4), as Proposition 1 predicts.
- **per_tx_policy** additionally admits every repeated sale (B1): 48 of 64 repeats.
- **Exfiltration:** every guarded layer refused all 80 exfiltration attempts. The target, method and recipient caveats are enough for those. Without on-chain controls, 64 attempts moved funds to the attacker.
- **Ablations:**
  - Floors alone admit 59 of 64 repeats.
  - Order and once-only alone admit every reserve breach, goal miss, budget overspend and wrong-asset swap (84 in total). The goal floor is what pins the output asset.
  - Both enforcers are needed.
- **Gas** (calibrated, same state across layers):
  - plan_guard's sale costs the same as LimitedCalls (about +49k over the built-in per-transaction caveats).
  - Its step 2 is about 56k cheaper than the built-in plan's (388,894 against 444,973 on D1). Each floor reads a balance once, after the execution; each relative balance-change caveat stores the balance before and compares it after.

## Limits

- **Constructed actions, not recorded calldata.** The recorded failures B1 and B2 are replayed by shape (value and order).
- **One DEX route and one chain:** Uniswap V2 on Ethereum mainnet at one block.
- **Single redemptions only.** Within a batched redemption, prerequisite checks follow the delegation manager's hook order.
- **Not audited.** The enforcers have not had a security audit.
- **Attempt files.** `matrix-v2/*.attempt*.json` are earlier attempts, kept as recorded:
  - an oracle mislabel (B2 counted off-plan when it met the constraints);
  - an H1 crash (decimals);
  - pre-fix runs whose code differs only in the H1 decimals path.
